1. Chromatography — core facts

Soluble in the solvent

Moves up the paper.

MOVES
Insoluble in the solvent

Stays on the start line.

STAYS
Pencil line

Pencil is graphite, not lead. Graphite is insoluble in the solvent.

DOES NOT MOVE
Same dye

The same dye moves the same distance in the same solvent.

SAME DISTANCE
Exam trap: every reason is about soluble or insoluble in the solvent. Never “element” or “compound”.
Also remember:
• Ink is soluble in the solvent.
• The solvent level is below the start line.

2. Chromatography — write in 3 lines

What you see — what the chromatogram shows.
Why — soluble, insoluble, or same distance.
So — what happens next or what it means.
The “So” line must say something new. Do not repeat the “Why” line.
Which dye is in the sample?
What you see
In both solvents, the spot of F moved the same distance as the spot of K.
Why
The same dye moves the same distance in the same solvent.
So
So F contains dye K.
Why is the start line drawn in pencil?
What you see
Pencil is made of graphite.
Why
Graphite is insoluble in the solvent.
So
So the line does not move up the paper and mix with the spots.
What if the line is drawn in ink?
What you see
The line is drawn in ink.
Why
The ink is soluble in the solvent.
So
So the ink moves up the paper and mixes with the spots.
Why is this solvent unsuitable?
What you see
The spot of ink 4 stayed on the start line.
Why
Ink 4 is insoluble in water.
So
So water cannot separate the dyes in ink 4.

3. Distillation — core facts

Solution, e.g. salt water

Only the water boils. The solid stays in the flask.

WATER BOILS
Two liquids

The one boiling is the liquid whose boiling point matches the thermometer.

MATCH TEMPERATURE
Cold water

Cools the vapour so that it condenses back to liquid.

VAPOUR → LIQUID
Collected liquid

The liquid collected is called the distillate.

DISTILLATE
Exam trap: the cold water cools the vapour, not “the condenser”.

4. Condenser sequence

The vapour goes into the inner tube.
Cold water flows around the inner tube. It never touches the vapour.
The cold water cools the vapour, so it condenses to liquid.
The liquid drips out. This is the distillate.
